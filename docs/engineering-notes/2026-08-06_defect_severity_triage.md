## The rubric

**Axis 1 — Consequence.** Worst that applies.
`M` money: can cause, misdirect, mis-size, or prevent a real trade or fund movement. `T` truth: displays or records something false the operator acts on. `D` data: can lose or corrupt persisted state. `S` stability: crash, hang, leak, material degradation. `F` function: feature does not work, but fails visibly and safely. `C` cosmetic.

**Axis 2 — Reachability.** `1` automatic (timer, boot, normal tick). `2` routine operator action. `3` occasional, a specific but legitimate sequence. `4` rare, unusual sequence or error condition.

**Axis 3 — Detectability.** `a` silent. `b` logged only, where the operator does not watch. `c` visible.

**Bands.**
- **P0** = (M or T) at reachability 1–2 with detectability a–b; also D at reachability 1–2 with detectability a–b.
- **P1** = M or D at any reachability/detectability not already P0; also T at reachability 3 with detectability a.
- **P2** = T or S at routine reachability with visible detectability; or M/D that is genuinely rare and visible.
- **P3** = F at any reachability; S that is rare or visible.
- **P4** = C.

**Two rulings this triage applies, because the graded set was inconsistent without them.**

1. *"Sim-only" and "developer tooling" are not axes and are not band discounts.* The distinction is already priced at Axis 1: a falsehood that cannot reach an order grades T, never M. Discounting a second time at band derivation double-counts. C18 was escalated on exactly this ground; C25, C27, C34, C44 and C19 were held or lowered on it. Where I could not eliminate the inconsistency I say so in section 6 rather than paper over it.

2. *Where the mechanical bands conflict with the calibration anchors, the anchors govern, and the operative distinction is who generates the falsehood.* Mechanically T-2a is P0, yet the C05 anchor puts T-2a at P1. The reconciling fact is that C05's falsehood is produced by a state-changing operator click, while NF-5's and C51's are produced continuously by the machine while the operator merely reads. So: machine-generated falsehood on a passively-read surface counts as reachability 1 and lands P0; falsehood produced by the operator's own state-changing action counts as reachability 2 and lands P1. Opening a tab is passive consumption, not a state-changing action.

## Systemic observations

**The audit was organised by surface, and the defect that matters most runs across all of them.** The risk tiers are gui-truth, sim-only, live-behaviour, record-only and perf. The compounding chain runs smart_wire to tranche build to target growth to GUI read-out, belonging to no tier, so 56 surface passes found none of it. A mechanism-oriented pass over the accumulation chain produced three items in one sitting, two of them new and one of them P0 with live stranded dollars.

**"Park it in a bucket for later" is a repeating pattern with a repeating bug: the outlet is missing or one-shot, and a log line or docstring affirmatively promises the drain.** `_pending_wire_credits` has a one-shot outlet gated on `_tranche_count_before == 0`. `_standing_surplus_usd` has no outlet at all: the only writes in `src/` are `+=` at `scrumming_bot.py:1393` and `:1415`, while `:789-799` records the operator's directive that standing surplus must be spread out over time and `bot_container.py:826-830` claims the budget "drains it into target_balance over time". Neither is true, and `standing_surplus` appears nowhere in `src/gui/`, so the bucket is not even displayed. The deferred-tranche queue at `:7960-7988` is the same shape and was not verified. Recommended sweep: for every accumulator field on ScrummingBot, grep for a decrement. Absence of a `-=` or a reset is the defect signature.

**The P0 queue is ordered by mechanism, not by what is firing.** All 35 live bots quote USD or USDC, and `scrumming_bot.py:2459-2461` hard-sets `_quote_to_usd = 1.0` for stable quotes; every persisted `quote_to_usd` is exactly 1.0. C39a, C39b and C39c are all missing `_quote_to_usd` conversions, so all three are arithmetically inert today and arm the instant the operator creates one ETH- or BTC-quoted bot. Meanwhile DOCKET-target-delta is firing on 24 of 35 rows this second, C09 is dropping 24 of 40 wires on every repaint, and C60 is aborting 2 bots' ticks continuously. Every M and T item needs a firing-today / latent flag resolved against the state file before the remediation order is fixed.

**Compounding does fire; it is capped, then drained into sinks, then hidden three ways.** Fleet-wide accrued growth is $26.20, which refutes a literal reading of "compounding has never worked at any point". The accurate statement is that growth fires, is capped at 1% of anchor per cycle, loses everything above the cap to `_standing_surplus_usd` which never drains, loses routed wire income to `_pending_wire_credits` whose outlet is gated shut, and then reports none of it: target growth is invisible because the GUI reads the frozen config value, the standing-surplus sink is absent from `get_status()` entirely, and Inflow/Outflow are hard $0.00 on every row. Three sinks and three blind read-outs explain the operator's experience with no single dramatic bug, and that is exactly why per-item grading missed it.

**Capacity constants are never checked against the operator's actual state, and every one truncates silently.** `LANE_COUNT = 8` against 40 saved wires. `api_logger` `max_entries = 500` against a 600 CPM ceiling. `idempotency` `max_entries = 5000` on a process-wide cache a Fleet Replay can flood. `_MAX_POINTS = 500` in sim_visuals. In all four, hitting the cap is indistinguishable from "there was nothing more", and in all four the grade was derived from code reachability alone. Reading `~/.acervator/*.json` settled two open questions in minutes. Inspecting live state should be a standard verification step for any capacity or geometry constant.

**Runtime state reads are decisive and must be dated.** C15 reports 16,558 bot_ids and a 6.5 MB residue in `reservation_state.json`; C16 reports 28 reservations and zero cross-bot ownership, and builds a demotion on it. Both are true: a purge ran on 2026-08-03, three days before this triage, and the quarantine files are still on disk. C16's demotion is correct only for the post-purge tree, and its escalation trigger was a live condition on ETH, USDT and BTC until 72 hours ago. Any finding resting on a runtime file needs the file's mtime recorded beside the number.

**"Silent" is being used for two different failure shapes and they are not equivalent.** Omissions (C09's unpainted wire, C42's null fixture, C29's dropped marker, C54's $0.00) leave a hole a suspicious operator might notice. Affirmative lies (C44's green tick, C56's `verify()` returning True, C19's `status=FILLED` at a price the market never traded, C45's CPM pill, C39a's "All other gates passed" on a bot at ceiling, C24's trade count rendered as dollars) actively terminate the search. The rubric names the second class explicitly and both P0 anchors belong to it. Several demotions treated the two as the same.

**Test suites are pinning the silence as specification.** `tests/test_bot_swarm_list.py:247-258` asserts the C09 drop with the comment "wire silently skipped". `tests/test_c14_telemetry_isolation.py` pins an override mechanism nothing in production sets. The C60 guard test was retired to `_archive` at the same time its method was deleted. A defect with a green test defending it survives every future review. Any fix for these must invert or delete the pin in the same commit, and the docket should carry that as an explicit sub-task.

**Seven safety and maintenance subsystems exist, are believed present, and have zero callers.** `prune_expired`, `StoneTabletAutoUpdater`, `call_with_breaker`, `gate_healer`, `NuclearFleetController.set_swarm_hooks`, `AnalyticsTab`, and RiskManager's PAUSE_ALL/PAUSE_BOT enforcement. Individually each grades P3 or P4 and each demotion is correct; collectively the operator believes he has a risk manager that pauses bots, a circuit breaker, a reservation reaper, an archive updater and a gate healer, and has none of them, with nothing anywhere reporting "subsystem X has never run". Recommend one cross-cutting item: a boot-time or on-demand wired-subsystems report, rather than seven independent P3s that will each be deferred on their own merits. `src/trading/profit_fold.py` belongs on the same list: it declares itself the single source of truth for target-balance growth across all three engines, has zero production callers, and implements a different formula from the live path (it includes the ADR-024 entry-price ref adjustment and the portfolio-value ceiling that `_apply_fold_target_growth` omits).

**Several demotions rest on silent backstops that are one line from re-arming.** C39a is held below P0 by `_execute_buy` Layer 2, which is gated on `position_ceiling_enabled`, False on all 35 live bots; Layer 1 is a deliberate no-op on the fold-rebuy and manual-tranche-fire paths (`scrumming_bot.py:4275-4285`), so those buy paths currently have no position-level cap at all and no triaged item covers that. C16 is held at P3 by the operator happening to run 35 distinct assets. C19's Fleet half is held safe by a `raise`. C55 is held inert by an `@property` shadowing a dataclass field of the same name. C15 is held safe by a never-raises wrapper. None of these emits anything when it catches something, so the day a backstop stops working, nothing changes on screen. Any demotion resting on a silent backstop should ship an invariant test naming it.

**One coupling nobody noticed:** `guarded_place_order` returns from the VolumeGuard branch (`bot_container.py:1027-1048`) *before* the idempotency block (`:1063-1076`). Re-enabling VolumeGuard sends every order with no client_order_id and a synthetic `vg_<ms>` id. That links C55 to C17, and neither item records it.
