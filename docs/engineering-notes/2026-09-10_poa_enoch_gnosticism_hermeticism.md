## 0. The sourcing rule

**HIS.** The rule is the source layer. Blending passes. Blending from somebody
else's interpretation fails.

> "Then this blending is acceptable. Just want authenticity from the originating
> sources rather than remixes and poor interpretations."

> "Not interested in anything Crowley or beyond the old, verified texts. That is OTO
> again and they just blended shit together however they felt they needed to in order
> to summon demons and gain power."

**HIS.** The test every later unit inherits.

```
admissible   a work that reads the originating texts and synthesises from them,
             and which then enters the record as a source in its own right
excluded     a work built from summaries, from translations of translations, and
             from other interpreters, adding correspondences that have no
             originating text
```

**MINE.** A date works as a rough test and never replaces the rule. Éliphas Lévi in the
1850s is where the interpretation layer starts producing more material than the
source layer. Where the date and the rule disagree, the rule wins, and section 8
gives a case where they do disagree.

```
the proxy    nothing after Eliphas Levi, Dogme et Rituel de la Haute Magie,
             1854-1856
excluded     Levi, Mathers, Westcott, Blavatsky, Regardie, Crowley, Kenneth
             Grant, Dion Fortune, and everything downstream, including the
             correspondence tables and the qliphothic tunnel systems
admitted     Corpus Hermeticum, Asclepius, the Emerald Tablet, Zosimos, the
             Jabirian corpus, the Turba Philosophorum, Ripley's gates,
             Paracelsus, Basil Valentine, the Rosarium, the Greek Magical
             Papyri, 1 Enoch, the Zohar, Lurianic Kabbalah
```

**SOURCED.** Agrippa is the case the design depends on, and he passes on the source
layer rather than on the date. He worked from the texts.

```
Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa, De occulta philosophia libri tres
  draft 1510, printed Cologne 1533

his sources, per the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry
  Ficino     De vita; the commentaries on Plato and on Plotinus
  Pico       Oratio; Conclusiones; Heptaplus; Disputationes
  Reuchlin   De verbo mirifico, 1494; De arte cabalistica, 1517
  also       Francesco Giorgio, De harmonia mundi; the Picatrix;
             pseudo-Albertus, Liber de mirabilibus mundi; Pliny,
             Naturalis historia; manuscripts from Trithemius
  and        the Hermetic and Neoplatonic texts themselves
```

**MINE.** The Sephirot pass and the qliphothic tunnels fail. Agrippa
read the originating material and synthesised from it, which is what the rule asks.
The tunnels come from the interpretation layer and carry no originating text. The
1533 date agrees with that verdict and does not produce it.

**MINE.** One half of the standard is attribution. Where the design takes a later
synthesiser, it names which originating texts that person worked from. Taking
Agrippa means saying Ficino, Pico and Reuchlin out loud.

## 1. Zosimos and the Watchers — the load-bearing lead holds, and it reverses

**SOURCED.** Zosimos of Panopolis cites the Watcher story. The passage survives in a
Byzantine chronicle quoting him, not in the surviving alchemical manuscripts.

```
George Syncellus, Ecloga Chronographica, c. 810
  quoting Zosimos of Panopolis, Imouth book 9, addressed to Theosebeia

  "It is stated in the holy scriptures or books, dear lady, that there exists a
   race of daimons who have commerce with women. Hermes made mention of them in
   his Physika; in fact almost the entire work, openly and secretly, alludes to
   them."

  "It is related in the ancient and divine scriptures that certain angels lusted
   for women, and descending from the heavens, they taught them all the arts of
   nature. On account of this, says the scripture, they offended god, and now
   live outside heaven - because they taught to men all the evil arts which are
   of no advantage to the soul."

  rendered in Kyle Fraser, "Zosimos of Panopolis and the Book of Enoch: Alchemy
  as Forbidden Knowledge", Aries 4.2 (2004), 125-147
```

**SOURCED.** An older rendering of the same fragment carries the naming of the art.

```
Syncellus, quoting Zosimos

  "The ancient and divine writings say that the angels became enamoured of
   women; and, descending, taught them all the works of nature. From them,
   therefore, is the first tradition, chema, concerning these arts; for they
   called this book chema and hence the science of chemistry takes its name."
```

**The reversal, and the most useful finding on this page.** Zosimos does not
make the Watchers the rightful origin of alchemy. He sets two lineages against each
other and the Watchers hold the corrupt one.

```
Christian H. Bull, "Wicked Angels and the Good Demon: The Origins of Alchemy
According to the Physica of Hermes", Gnosis: Journal of Gnostic Studies 3.1 (2018)

  Zosimos harmonises 1 Enoch with the Physica of Hermes
  the Watchers   wicked angels who PERVERTED the art
  the authentic  revealed to Hermes by Chemeu, identified with Agathodaimon
  Zosimos is the only source for Hermes's Physica, which is lost
```

**MINE.** The design gets a two-lineage structure straight from its own central
figure. One art, two transmissions, one of them stolen and taught for gain. That is
better than a single origin myth because it supplies a conflict without needing a
devil.

## 2. What 1 Enoch 7 and 8 actually teach — the lead holds, with one correction

**SOURCED.** Chapter 6 carries the descent and the oath. Chapter 7 carries the wives,
the first teaching and the giants. Chapter 8 carries the curriculum by named angel.

```
1 Enoch 7:1, R. H. Charles translation

  "And all the others together with them took unto themselves wives, and each
   chose for himself one, and they began to go in unto them..."

  the teaching in the same verse: charms, enchantments, the cutting of roots,
  and acquaintance with plants
```

**SOURCED.** Chapter 8 is the catalogue, and it names each teacher.

```
1 Enoch 8:1, Charles

  "And Azazel taught men to make swords, and knives, and shields, and
   breastplates, and made known to them the metals <of the earth> and the art of
   working them, and bracelets, and ornaments, and the use of antimony, and the
   beautifying of the eyelids, and all kinds of costly stones, and all colouring
   tinctures."

1 Enoch 8:3, Charles

  "Semjaza taught enchantments, and root-cuttings, Armaros the resolving of
   enchantments, Baraqijal (taught) astrology, Kokabel the constellations,
   Ezeqeel the knowledge of the clouds, <Araqiel the signs of the earth,
   Shamsiel the signs of the sun>, and Sariel the course of the moon."
```

**The correction.** The lead called this a catalogue of the hermetic arts. The text
names metallurgy, weapons, cosmetics, gemstones, dyes, enchantment, the undoing of
enchantment, root-cutting, astrology and the celestial signs. The chapter names no
alchemy and no transmutation. Zosimos applies the hermetic reading from outside.

**SOURCED.** Fraser identifies the hinge. The colouring tinctures of 8:1 are what
would have signalled alchemy to an alchemist reading the chapter.

**MINE.** Nine named teachers with nine named subjects is a usable roster on its own.
The roster also carries a warning the design should keep: in its own text this
curriculum is a crime, not a gift.

## 3. Panopolis and Akhmim — the lead understated it

**SOURCED.** Zosimos worked at Panopolis, which is modern Akhmim, around 300. The
Greek manuscript of 1 Enoch carries the same place in its name.

```
Codex Panopolitanus, the Akhmim fragment, P. Cair. 10759

  contents    Greek 1 Enoch 1:1-32:6, the Gospel of Peter, and fragments of the
              Apocalypse of Peter
  found       1886-87, a Christian grave at Akhmim, French mission
  published   Urbain Bouriant, 1892
  dated       sixth century or later; some descriptions say eighth or later
  provenance  insecure; the monastic burial is Bouriant's postulate, with no
              independent evidence
```

**SOURCED.** Scholarship does draw something from it, and one study makes the link
directly.

```
Daniel C. Olson, "From the Alchemist's Library? Zosimos of Panopolis and Codex
Panopolitanus", Henoch 35 (2013), 135-153

  the claim   glosses in the Greek 1 Enoch of this codex may plausibly be traced
              to Zosimos of Panopolis or to his students
  the use     knowing the provenance helps text-critical work on the manuscript,
              including the detection of earlier glosses
```

**MINE.** Hold this at its real size. The article's own title is a question. One
scholar proposes it, the claim covers marginal glosses, the manuscript postdates
Zosimos by centuries, and the evidence points at a library tradition rather than at a
book he held. Do not say Zosimos owned this codex. Say that a published study proposes his
circle left marks in its text, and that the two names point at one town.

## 4. Nag Hammadi Codex VI — the lead holds, with one correction

**SOURCED.** The codex is real, dates to the mid-fourth century, and carries three
hermetic works.

```
Nag Hammadi Codex VI, mid-fourth century Coptic

  VI,1   The Acts of Peter and the Twelve Apostles
  VI,2   The Thunder, Perfect Mind
  VI,3   Authoritative Teaching
  VI,4   The Concept of Our Great Power
  VI,5   Plato, Republic 588b-589b
  VI,6   The Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth
  VI,7   The Prayer of Thanksgiving, with a scribal note
  VI,8   Asclepius 21-29
```

**SOURCED.** The Prayer of Thanksgiving survives in three witnesses at once, and the
three sit in three different bodies of literature.

```
Latin    Asclepius 41, inside the philosophical dialogue
Coptic   Nag Hammadi Codex VI,7
Greek    PGM III.591-611, inside a magical handbook
```

**The correction.** The lead put the hermetic texts in one binding with gnostic texts.
Codex VI holds a mixture rather than a tidy pair. One item is Plato, one is an
apocryphal Christian act, and scholars contest the labels on the rest. Say the codex
carries hermetic, Christian and Platonic material in one binding.

**MINE.** This is still the documentary evidence the design wanted. One fourth-century
community copied a hermetic prayer, a hermetic initiation dialogue and an extract of
the Asclepius into the same book as the rest.

## 5. The doctrinal split — both halves hold, and neither side is uniform

**SOURCED.** The sharply anticosmic gnostic position is in the text. The maker of the
world claims to be the only god and the text calls that ignorance.

```
Apocryphon of John, NHC II,1, 11,19-22

  "For he said, 'I am God and there is no other God beside me,' for he is
   ignorant of his strength, the place from which he had come."

  the archon's three names: Yaltabaoth, Saklas, Samael
```

**SOURCED.** The philosophical hermetica affirm the cosmos, and they do it in the
strongest available terms.

```
Corpus Hermeticum VIII.2, Mead 1906

  "Second is he 'after His image', Cosmos, brought into being by Him, sustained
   and fed by Him, made deathless, as by his own Sire, living for aye, as ever
   free from death."

Corpus Hermeticum XI.6, Mead 1906

  "Regard its Beauty carefully - Body in pure perfection, though one than which
   there's no more ancient one, ever in prime of life, and ever-young."

Asclepius 10

  there are two images of God, Cosmos and man
  whoever maintains the cosmos's beauty and increases it joins his own work to
  the will of God
```

**SOURCED.** The hermetica are not uniform. One treatise takes the opposite side.

```
Corpus Hermeticum VI.4, Mead 1906
  titled "In God Alone is Good and Elsewhere Nowhere"

  "For that the world is 'fullness' of the bad, but God of Good, and Good of
   God."

also world-rejecting
  Corpus Hermeticum VII    the body as a garment of ignorance
  Corpus Hermeticum XIII   twelve tormentors of matter, driven out by ten powers
```

**SOURCED.** The division inside the hermetica is a recognised problem, not a
discovery. Festugière separated an optimist gnosis from a pessimist one inside the
same corpus.

```
A.-J. Festugiere, La Revelation d'Hermes Trismegiste, 1944-1954
```

**SOURCED.** The gnostic side is not uniform either, and the leading study argues the
label itself does not fit.

```
Michael Allen Williams, Rethinking "Gnosticism": An Argument for Dismantling a
Dubious Category, Princeton University Press, 1996

  a chapter titled "Anticosmic World-Rejection? or Sociocultural Accommodation?"
  the argument: no single trait set - divine spark, evil demiurge, anticosmism -
  fits all the texts the label collects
  his replacement pair: a negatively valued demiurge separated from the high God,
  and engagement with biblical tradition

Karen L. King, What Is Gnosticism?, Harvard University Press, 2003
```

**MINE.** The word most carried real weight in the lead, and the lead survives with
its edges named. The anticosmic demiurge is the sharpest gnostic position and the
texts do carry it. The cosmos-affirming position dominates the hermetica and takes a
dissenting treatise inside its own corpus.

## 6. The relationship's shape — the lead holds, with one refinement

**SOURCED.** The standard account puts both in one milieu and denies a clean descent.

```
Roelof van den Broek, "Gnosticism and Hermetism in Antiquity: Two Roads to
Salvation", in van den Broek and Hanegraaff, eds, Gnosis and Hermeticism from
Antiquity to Modern Times, SUNY Press, 1998

  the similarities follow from both originating and developing at the same time,
  in the same eastern part of the Mediterranean, and in the same spiritual
  climate
```

**SOURCED.** The same scholar states the agreement and the disagreement in one place.

```
Roelof van den Broek, "Hermetism and Gnosticism", chapter 5 in Glenn Alexander
Magee, ed., The Cambridge Handbook of Western Mysticism and Esotericism,
Cambridge University Press, 2016

  both flourished in the Greco-Roman world of the first centuries of our era
  both proclaimed a salvific knowledge, gnosis, meant for an elite
  they agree on human origin and destiny
  they differ on "the nature of the material world and the manner of salvation"

  "To a certain extent, Gnosticism shows a radicalization of ideas that are also
   present, though not dominant or structural, in Hermetism."
```

**The refinement.** The lead said neither derives from the other. That holds for
descent. One scholar names a direction of intensification, and it runs from hermetism
to gnosticism rather than the reverse. No scholar on this page derives hermetism from
gnosticism.

**SOURCED.** The scholars to name, and what each supplies.

```
Festugiere     Greek philosophical reading; the optimist and pessimist split
Garth Fowden   The Egyptian Hermes, Cambridge 1986, Princeton 1993; the social
               and Egyptian setting
Jean-Pierre Mahe  "Gnostic and Hermetic Ethics", in the SUNY volume above;
               editor of the Coptic hermetica
van den Broek  the two-roads account, and the handbook chapter
Christian H. Bull  The Tradition of Hermes Trismegistus, Brill 2018; Egyptian
               priestly brotherhoods teaching a Greek-speaking audience
Wouter Hanegraaff  Hermetic Spirituality and the Historical Imagination,
               Cambridge 2022; the Hermetica as a practised spirituality
Williams, King  the dismantling of "Gnosticism" as a category
```

## 8. The rule catches something the date does not

**SOURCED.** The tarot is a fifteenth-century north Italian card game, and for about
four centuries it served only as a game.

```
first documentary evidence   Ferrara court records, 1442
earliest surviving decks     Visconti-Sforza, Cary-Yale, Brambilla
purpose                      play; divinatory and occult use is unknown before
                             the eighteenth century

Michael Dummett, The Game of Tarot, 1980
Ronald Decker, Thierry Depaulis and Michael Dummett, A Wicked Pack of Cards:
The Origins of the Occult Tarot, 1996
```

**SOURCED.** The tarot-Hebrew correspondence does not originate with Lévi. It
originates seventy-three years earlier, and it arrived with an invented Egyptian
pedigree attached.

```
Comte de Mellet, essay in Antoine Court de Gebelin, Le Monde primitif,
volume 8, 1781

  the first recorded link between the cards and the Hebrew letters
  his order ran backwards: trump XXI, The World = aleph, down to the Fool = tau
  de Gebelin claimed an ancient Egyptian origin for the cards
  de Mellet derived "tarot" from an Egyptian "Ta Rosh", the science of Thoth

Eliphas Levi, Dogme et Rituel de la Haute Magie, 1854-1856

  22 chapters per volume, against the 22 trumps
  fixed the forward mapping every later system uses - the Juggler = aleph,
  the Papess = beth
  tied the cards to the paths of the Tree of Life
```

**MINE.** This is the clearest single illustration of his rule, and it works because
the rule and the date disagree. A cutoff at Lévi admits 1781. The rule excludes it,
because the correspondence has no originating text and its authority claim — ancient
Egypt — is itself invented.

```
the failure mode the rule exists to catch

  material that reads as traditional, carries an ancient attribution, and has no
  originating text behind it
```

**SOURCED.** A second case, and the design already uses the text it corrupts. The
popular slogan is a modern paraphrase, and the Emerald Tablet's earliest witness is
neither Egyptian nor ancient.

```
the Latin vulgate, Chrysogonus Polydorus edition, 1541

  "Quod est inferius est sicut quod est superius, et quod est superius est sicut
   quod est inferius, ad perpetranda miracula rei unius."

  the last clause: "to accomplish the miracles of the one thing"
  "as above, so below" is a modern paraphrase that drops that clause

earliest attestation
  Arabic, late eighth or early ninth century, in Kitab sirr al-khaliqa,
  the Book of the Secret of Creation, presented as a work of Apollonius of
  Tyana, Balinas in Arabic
  Latin from the twelfth century: Hugo of Santalla, c. 1145-1151; the vulgate,
  first half of the twelfth century; Philip of Tripoli, thirteenth century,
  inside the Secret of Secrets
```

**MINE.** The operative half is the half the slogan drops. The Tablet states a
correspondence and then names its purpose. Take the full clause and the line becomes
a procedure rather than a motto.

## 9. On the revival's purpose, answered rather than agreed with

**HIS.** His charge is that the revival blended material "in order to summon demons
and gain power".

**SOURCED.** The stated purposes are not that. Lévi's is a reconciliation, and he
warned readers off practice.

```
Eliphas Levi, Dogme et Rituel de la Haute Magie, 1854-1856

  magic placed between science and religion, with a mediating function
  a universal secret doctrine held to exist throughout history everywhere
  the reader is repeatedly cautioned against practice; called dangerous and
  hallucinatory without mental discipline
  one evocation attempted, of Apollonius of Tyana, with Bulwer-Lytton, London
  1854; afterwards condemned as dangerous on health and moral grounds except
  for an experienced adept
```

**SOURCED.** Crowley's stated aim is a theurgic one, and the Golden Dawn supplied the
term.

```
the aim      the Great Work, and the Knowledge and Conversation of the Holy
             Guardian Angel; begun 1900, attained by his own account 1906
the term     Mathers styled it so in his 1897 translation of The Book of the
             Sacred Magic of Abramelin the Mage
```

**SOURCED.** The demon-evocation material they published is older than they are.

```
The Goetia, book one of the Lemegeton, or Lesser Key of Solomon

  a seventeenth-century compilation from earlier sources
  its demon list overlaps Johann Weyer, Pseudomonarchia Daemonum, 1577
  its procedures appear in Reginald Scot, The Discoverie of Witchcraft, 1584
  Mathers and Crowley published their edition in 1904
```

**MINE.** The charge as phrased is a reputation rather than a description. The
revival edited and printed pre-modern grimoire literature; it did not invent demon
evocation, and its own stated goals are self-transformation and contact with a
guardian angel.

**MINE.** The accurate charge is different and stronger, because his own rule already
tests it. The revival published unsourced correspondences as ancient
transmission. That is what section 8 measures, it needs no claim about anyone's
motives, and it excludes the same authors on evidence.

## What a design must not claim

**MINE.** Each line is a claim these sources do not support.

```
do not claim

  that Zosimos treats the Watchers as alchemy's rightful origin
      he treats them as the corrupt branch; the authentic line is Chemeu to
      Hermes

  that 1 Enoch 7-8 catalogues alchemy
      it catalogues metallurgy, cosmetics, dyes, enchantment, root-cutting and
      astrology; the alchemical reading is Zosimos's

  that Zosimos owned or read Codex Panopolitanus
      the manuscript is centuries later; the published claim concerns glosses
      traced to him or his students

  that Nag Hammadi Codex VI is a gnostic codex with hermetic texts in it
      it carries hermetic, Christian and Platonic material in one binding

  that all gnostic systems are anticosmic
      Williams 1996 dismantles the uniformity; Valentinian material differs

  that the hermetica are uniformly cosmos-affirming
      CH VI.4 calls the world a fullness of the bad

  that either body descends from the other
      parallel currents in one milieu; one direction of intensification is
      named, hermetism to gnosticism

  that the tarot-Hebrew correspondence is ancient, or Levi's invention
      1781, Comte de Mellet, with no originating text behind it

  that the revival's purpose was summoning demons for power
      the stated aims are theurgic; the evocation material is pre-modern
      grimoire literature they reprinted
```
