# Take the widest true reading

**The acceptance bar goes where the directive is, not where the proof is
cheapest.**

Operator, 2026-09-06: *"Why are you so eager to do less work!!!!! AIM HIGHER!!!"*

## The shape

A directive names an outcome. Writing the brief means turning that outcome into
something checkable. **That translation is where the work gets lost** — not in
laziness, and not in misunderstanding. Every narrowing below was defensible,
provable, and wrong.

| he said | the narrow reading | what it cost |
|---|---|---|
| convert the GUI to React **and Electron** | "renders under `ACERVATOR_VARIANT=react`" | 46 rows marked, 16 reaching the shell |
| a debugger after every code edit | "for the conversion's screens" | a screens-only tool, nearly shipped |
| the entire GUI | "the rows the running window builds" | 33 of 76 rows dropped as out of scope |
| verify the conversion | "the canonical check passes" | no row checked against the Qt screen |

**One directive, two readings, and the cheaper one always won.** That is the
defect this skill exists to stop.

## The three questions, before a brief goes out

```
1  What is the WIDEST reading of this that is still true to his words?
2  What would the unit have to show for THAT reading to be proved?
3  Is that what my acceptance criterion asks for — or something smaller?
```

Question 3 is the skill. When the answer is "something smaller", the brief is
rewritten, not sent.

## The tells of a narrowed bar

A brief has narrowed when its proof rests on any of these:

```
a SUBSET      one tab, one variant, one host, when he named the whole
a PROXY HOST  the screen drew somewhere, not where he runs it
a GREEN       the check passed, rather than the thing worked
a CATEGORY    "in scope" decided by me, excluding what he never excluded
a LATER       "that part comes after", when he set no order
```

**"It is defensible" is the warning sign, not the defence.** A reading that
needs defending is the narrow one.

## When the wide reading is genuinely too large

Say so **in one line, before starting**, with the measurement that makes it
large. That is his call to make.

Never decide it silently by scoping the proof. A unit that finishes early
because its criterion was small has not finished, and reporting it as done is a
false report however green the gate.

## This applies to the referee first

The units do what the brief says. **The brief is where this fails**, so the
check runs on my own words before dispatch, not on their reports afterwards.

It applies equally to a claim of done, a table column, and a status line: each
one encodes a bar, and each one can encode a smaller bar than he set.

## Falsification

This skill is wrong if he asks for a narrower scope than the brief carries.
Then the narrowing came from him, which is the only place it may come from.
